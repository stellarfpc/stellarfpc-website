FIRE SAFETY FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Simple answers to common questions about Fire Safety Plans in Ontario.

What is a Fire Safety Plan?

A Fire Safety Plan is a building-specific document that outlines fire emergency procedures, supervisory staff responsibilities, fire drills, fire hazards, and information about the building's fire protection systems. For buildings subject to Section 2.8 of the Ontario Fire Code, the plan must be prepared, approved and implemented.

The Ontario Fire Code also requires the plan to be reviewed as often as necessary and at intervals of no more than 12 months.

Ontario Fire Code - Section 2.8

Does my building need a Fire Safety Plan?

It depends on the building's occupancy, size, occupant load and use. Section 2.8 of the Ontario Fire Code identifies the buildings and premises that require a Fire Safety Plan.

If you are unsure, send us the building address and basic occupancy information and we can help you identify the next step.

Can I prepare a Fire Safety Plan myself?

A building owner may prepare a Fire Safety Plan, but the document must accurately address the applicable Fire Code requirements and the actual building, fire protection systems and emergency procedures.

Many owners choose to use an experienced fire safety consultant because preparing the plan often requires reviewing building layouts, alarm and sprinkler systems, supervisory staff duties and emergency procedures.

What are the main steps to prepare a Fire Safety Plan?

The process normally includes reviewing the building information and existing documents, confirming the building layout and fire protection systems, preparing the Fire Safety Plan and floor plan information, submitting the plan for review when required, and responding to review comments.

The exact process depends on the building and the authority having jurisdiction.

Does a Fire Safety Plan need Fire Department approval?

For buildings regulated by Section 2.8 of the Ontario Fire Code, the Fire Safety Plan must be prepared, approved and implemented. The reviewing authority is generally the Chief Fire Official having jurisdiction.

Review timelines vary by municipality and by the complexity of the property.

Where should the Fire Safety Plan be kept?

The Ontario Fire Code requires the Fire Safety Plan to be kept in the building in an approved location.

The specific location may depend on the building and local requirements. Building management should ensure supervisory staff can access the plan when needed.

Do I need a Fire Safety Plan box?

A Fire Safety Plan box may be required or requested depending on the municipality and building.

Requirements for the box, location, keys and access can vary. Check the requirements of the local fire department or municipality before purchasing or installing one.

Do fire departments charge a fee to review a Fire Safety Plan?

Some Ontario municipalities charge fees for Fire Safety Plan review or related services, while others may not.

Fees and policies can change, so we recommend confirming the current requirements directly with the municipality or fire department having jurisdiction.
